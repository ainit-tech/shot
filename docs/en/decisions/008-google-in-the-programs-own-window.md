# Google in the program's own window

Google's pages open in a window of the program's own, made with WebView2, the engine of Edge built into Windows. The window keeps a profile of its own, where the user signs in to Google once, as in an ordinary browser, and the program can then drive the pages: put the screenshot and the question straight into AI Mode, which the user's own browser doesn't let it do. As it starts, the program finds out whether someone is signed in in the window and, if nobody is, invites to sign in; the user signs in from that invitation, from the page of a query or from the settings window. For "Ask Google" the window comes out next to the selection with AI Mode's conversation alone, to ask further; for "Translate" it stays hidden, and only the answer shows, in a card next to the selection; for "Search by image in Google" it comes out next to the selection with Google's results for the picture on the tab of exact matches. The window keeps a fresh AI Mode ready in the background, so that a question goes at once; held, "Ask Google" asks the user's own question aloud, and the microphone is on only while the button is held.

## Question

The user's own browser can't be driven from outside: the screenshot reaches AI Mode only through the results of Google Lens, and AI Mode itself is one click further ([decision](005-how-the-picture-reaches-a-search-engine.md)). How can a query land in AI Mode with the picture and the question at once, in the user's account?

## What is known (October 2026)

- AI Mode takes a picture that a script on its page pastes into the question field; the picture then shows in the field under the file's name. Checked in a browser on October 24, 2026.
- The button that sends the question is named `input-plate-send-button` in its `data-xid`, whatever the language of the page. Google enables it once the pasted picture has been uploaded, and a script can press it. A script that pasted a picture, typed the question and pressed the button got AI Mode's answer to the question about the picture. Checked in a browser on October 24, 2026.
- The address of a conversation from the window, opened in the user's default browser signed in to the same Google account, shows the question with an empty picture: the picture stays with the window's session even within one account. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- The answer of AI Mode is in the block named `main-col` in its `data-container-id`, inside the turn named `aim-mars-turn-root` in its `data-xid`. The parts of the answer are blocks with a `data-container-id` of their own; next to them Google may add a warning that AI can make mistakes. When the answer is complete, the block gets the attribute `data-sfc-inited`, at the same moment as the page tells screen readers that the response is ready. Checked in a browser on October 24, 2026.
- A picture pasted before the page's own script is ready isn't lost: Google keeps the event and hands it over later, so pasting again would attach the picture twice. Checked in a browser on October 24, 2026.
- A program that shows pages in WebView2 can run its own scripts on them, send forms from them and call the browser's developer protocol (DevTools), whose input pages receive as trusted events.
- Windows 11 includes WebView2. It names itself almost as Edge does and is updated together with Edge.
- Google refuses sign-in to Google accounts in browsers it takes for embedded in other programs: since 2019 in some browsers built on QtWebEngine (Falkon, qutebrowser, Konqueror), since 2021 in embedding libraries such as CEF. Its help names being embedded in another application among the reasons. How it treats WebView2 is reported differently.
- In a check, a window of WebView2 opened Google about a second after it was made. Google asked the user for a captcha there, and within a minute the cookies of the window were on disk in its profile. The user then signed in to Google in the window. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, the cookies of a fresh profile of the window, with AI Mode loaded and nobody signed in, had none of `SID`, `__Secure-1PSID` and `__Secure-3PSID`, which Google keeps for an account signed in. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- Windows holds the notifications of programs back while it takes some window for a full-screen program. On a computer with NVIDIA's overlay, the overlay keeps a window over the whole screen, and Windows reported a full-screen program all the time: the program's notification offering to sign in didn't show. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, the hidden window brought AI Mode's translation of a picture into the card about six seconds after the query, the start of WebView2 included. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- On AI Mode's page, Google's logo, the header with the other tabs and the account, and the rail with the history of conversations stand apart from the conversation: the header holds the block with the id `gb`, and the rail is named `m3fCN` in its `data-xid`. The page lays the conversation out on a grid whose first column, in a narrow window, is kept for the rail. Checked in a browser on October 24, 2026.
- The sources of AI Mode's answer open in a new tab, through Google's own address `/goto`. Checked in a browser on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, the window came out next to the selection, without Google's header and rail, and showed AI Mode's answer about the picture about five seconds after the query, the start of WebView2 included. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- Google's page of results for a picture is at least about 690 pixels wide: its header doesn't get narrower than 684. Under the search box it shows a large copy of the picture (`jsname="ZZZihe"`) and keeps room for it with a padding of the block `sfcnt`. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, the window came out next to the selection with the exact matches for Google's logo about four seconds after the query, the start of WebView2 included, and a click on a page found went to the user's own browser. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In the window, AI Mode's microphone uses the browser's own speech recognition (`webkitSpeechRecognition`), and only it: without it, AI Mode hides the microphone. WebView2 has no speech service behind it: the recognition stops with the error "network" about a second after it starts. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- Google's search box uses Google's own speech recognition, which sends the sound to Google's server (`speechs3proto2-pa.googleapis.com`), and it works in the window. Google's script registers it as the page starts, as the first entry of a list of recognisers. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, AI Mode in the window heard a recorded question, in English and in Russian, through Google's own recognition under the browser's name, and sent it by itself after a pause. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- After its send button, AI Mode stops the recognition and waits for it to end. The browser's recognition first gives the words already said and then ends; Google's own stops at once, without the words still on their way and without saying that it has ended. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, a question asked aloud went to the fresh AI Mode kept ready: the microphone was on 0.4 seconds after the question was asked, the window stayed hidden while it listened, and once the program said to stop, the question went with the screenshot and the window came out next to the selection. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- In a check, a recognition stopped at once when the question asked aloud was given up and when its page was hidden, and by itself about eight seconds after it had heard nothing. Checked on October 24, 2026.
- Google serves its check that one is not a robot as "too many requests" (HTTP 429), and WebView2 calls a page's load failed when the server answers with anything but success, though the page is there and its scripts run. In a check on a server of a test's own, the check's page had its document, and then its load ended as failed: the program used to give the query up at that moment, so when the check let the user through by itself and went on to AI Mode, nothing was left to ask, and the window came out with AI Mode empty. Checked on October 29, 2026.
- In WebView2, a page whose load a new one replaces once the page has begun to come ends its load as a success. A page whose connection breaks before it is whole ends its load as failed and never has its document; where nobody answers, WebView2 shows a page of its own error, whose document comes before the load ends as failed. Checked on October 29, 2026, on a server of a test's own.

## Options

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A window of the program's own, on WebView2 | Its own window next to the screenshot; the program controls the page fully; nothing to install | Google may refuse the sign-in; the scripts depend on how Google's pages are made |
| A real Edge with a profile of its own, driven through the developer protocol | Signs in as an ordinary Edge does | Edge's own window frame; a separate Edge to start and watch |
| The user's own browser, as now | Nothing new to build | AI Mode is one click away from the answer; the picture can't be put into it directly |
| An AI service's API with the answer in the program's window | Documented and stable | Not Google's AI Mode with its search; needs a key |

## Why

Only a browser that the program itself controls lets it put the screenshot into Google's own AI Mode, in the user's account. WebView2 gives the neatest window and is already in Windows, so it goes first; if Google refuses the sign-in in it, the next option is a real Edge.

## How it works

- The settings window has the section "Google account" ([decision](007-settings-in-a-file.md)). Its button opens Google's account page in the program's window: the sign-in when nobody is signed in there, the account itself when someone is. The menu of the icon in the notification area has no such item, since one signs in once or very rarely.
- Signing in is much to be wished for: in a window where nobody was signed in Google asked for a captcha, and AI Mode keeps conversations in the history of an account only. Whether someone is signed in, the program learns from the window's cookies: Google keeps `SID`, `__Secure-1PSID` and `__Secure-3PSID` for an account signed in. As it starts, once the window's WebView2 has started, the program looks, and if nobody is signed in, it shows a small card of its own by the icon in the notification area, "Sign in to Google", with "Later" and "Sign in", which opens the sign-in. While the presentation "How to use" is open, as on the first start, the card waits until it is closed: the presentation offers to sign in meanwhile, under its clip. Windows' own notification wouldn't do: Windows holds notifications back while it takes some window for a full-screen game and while the user doesn't want to be disturbed. The card doesn't take the keyboard and goes by itself after half a minute unless the mouse is on it. The program looks again when the window closes, where the user may have signed in or out, and as the settings window opens; while nobody is signed in, the settings window invites to sign in on its first page and marks its section "Google account".
- While nobody is signed in, the pages of queries in the window, AI Mode's and the results for a picture, show a small strip at the top, "You aren't signed in to Google", with "Sign in": the program hides Google's own header with its sign-in there. "Sign in" opens Google's sign-in, which comes back to the page after it. The strip knows from the cookie `SAPISID`, which Google gives its pages only for an account signed in; its cross hides it until the program ends.
- The window keeps its profile in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\ainitShot\browser`, so the sign-in stays between runs. Its cookies reach the disk within about half a minute and when the window closes; they are encrypted with a key that the user's Windows account protects.
- For the account the window opens in the middle of the monitor under the mouse, and for a query next to the selection; closing it only hides it. Its title is the page's.
- The window keeps a fresh AI Mode ready in the background: it loads one as the program starts, again once the window has been closed, and as the hotkey freezes the screen if the one kept ready is older than half an hour; a window in sight or a query under way is left as it is. A question for a conversation goes to it at once, and the window comes out with it. Without one, the window loads the query's page hidden and comes out once the page's document is there, so that no page from before shows while the new one loads; the image search does the same with its own page.
- A query button with `mode = "chat"`, "Ask Google" among the first buttons, opens AI Mode (`https://www.google.com/ai`) in the window, and the window comes out next to the selection. As soon as the page's document is there, the program runs its script on it, and every step of the script starts when the page is ready for it, with no fixed waits: when the question field appears, the script pastes the screenshot into it once, as Ctrl+V does; when the picture shows in the field, it types the question; when Google enables the send button, it presses it. Time limits only decide when to give up. No browser extension is needed.
- A button for a conversation held for more than 0.35 seconds asks the user's own question aloud in place of its mini prompt. The screenshot goes into AI Mode in the hidden window while the microphone goes on; the frozen screen says "Говори" next to the button and then shows the words as they are heard. Once the button is let go, the page waits for the words already said, puts them into the question field and sends the question, and the window comes out next to the selection with the conversation. Pressed for a moment, or clicked, the button asks its mini prompt as before. Esc gives the question asked aloud up and keeps the screenshot; when nothing is heard, the frozen screen says so and nothing goes.
- A query button with `mode = "answer"`, "Translate" among the first buttons, asks AI Mode in the same way with the window hidden. Once the question has gone, the script hands the program the parts of the answer as they come, without Google's warning, until Google marks the answer complete, and the program shows them in a card next to the selection. Chromium slows down pages it takes for unseen, so the program starts WebView2 with that turned off.
- A query button with `mode = "images"`, "Search by image in Google" among the first buttons, opens Google Images (`https://images.google.com/`) in the window, and the window comes out next to the selection. The script sends the screenshot to Google Lens as the camera of Google Images sends a picture: at most 1024 pixels on its longer side, as JPEG on white, with the sizes of the picture and of the window (`ep=gsbubb`). Google opens its results on the tab "All"; before they load, the program opens them on the tab "Exact matches" instead: the pages with this very picture, whose titles often say who or what is in it, which the AI often gets wrong from a photo. The other tabs are a click away.
- The card goes to the right of the selection, level with its top, so that the text it translates stays in sight; to the left of it if there is no room on the right; under or above it if there is no room beside it; and it never leaves the screen. It is as wide as the answer's longest line, from 240 to 560 pixels at the scale of 100%, since longer lines are hard to read, and as tall as the text, up to 480 pixels or 60% of the screen's height; past that the text scrolls.
- The window goes next to the selection as the card does. At the scale of 100%, title bar included, it is 600 by 760 pixels for a conversation: enough for AI Mode's answers with their lists and sources and for the field for the next question, with lines that are still easy to read. For the image search's results it is 720 by 760, as wide as Google's page of results needs. On a smaller screen it takes as much of the screen as there is.
- Before the page's own scripts, the program hides Google's logo, the header with the other tabs and the account, and the rail with the history on AI Mode's page, and in a narrow window it gives the rail's column to the conversation: the window shows the conversation and the question field alone. On the results for a picture it hides the large copy of the picture under the search box, with the room kept for it: the picture itself is on the screen next to the window.
- AI Mode's microphone works in the window: before the page's own scripts, the program gives Google's pages a speech recognition under the browser's name, which hands the speech to Google's own recognition and its results back as the browser's would. The window asks for the microphone once; AI Mode sends the question by itself after a pause or by its send button. After the send button the program keeps Google's recognition on until the words already said have come, up to four seconds, and then ends it.
- The microphone never listens in the background. A question asked aloud has it on only while the button is held and until the words already said have come; the frozen screen closing or Esc turns it off at once, and a release over another window is noticed within a tenth of a second. Every recognition in the window ends when its page is hidden or left, as when the window is closed, after eight seconds without a word, and after a minute at most, and doesn't start in a hidden page. Without asking, the page may use the microphone only for a question asked aloud.
- A link that would open a new tab, a source of an answer for one, and a link that the user follows out of Google, a page found by the image search for one, open in the user's default browser.
- The card's text can be selected, and Ctrl+C with nothing selected copies all of it; Esc or a click elsewhere closes the card. "Show the conversation" under the text shows the hidden window next to the selection with the whole conversation, to ask more.
- If Google shows a page of its own first, its check that one is not a robot or of the account, the program gives it five seconds: often Google lets the user through by itself and goes on to the page asked for, where the script runs again, and for an answer nothing shows but the card. A page that stays waits for the user: the hidden window comes out with it instead of the card, and once the user is through, the query goes on in the window. A page that came, whatever the server answered with it, hasn't failed: the script on it tells how the query went.
- A query's page that doesn't come, or breaks off before it is whole, as one may over a connection that a pause has worn out, is asked for once more, as the user would ask again: nothing can have gone from such a page. If it doesn't come the second time either, the query gives up.
- If the script can't finish, it tells the program what stopped it; the program says so, puts the screenshot into the clipboard and shows the window, instead of the card for an answer, so that Ctrl+V finishes the query.

## Consequences

- The program depends on WebView2, which Windows 11 includes, and on Rust crates for its COM interfaces (`webview2-com`, `windows`).
- The window's engine runs from the program's start, with processes of its own and memory, and loads AI Mode in the background as the program starts and after the window is closed.
- The script depends on how AI Mode's page is made: the question field, the name of the send button, the name of the pasted file in the field, and the blocks and the mark of the answer. When Google changes them, the query stops at that step and says so.
- How the window tidies Google's pages depends on how they are made: on AI Mode's page, the id of the header, the name of the rail and the name of Google's style for the grid; on the results for a picture, the name of the copy of the picture and the id of the room kept for it. When Google changes them, what was hidden comes back, and the query still works.
- AI Mode's microphone depends on how Google's script registers its own speech recognition and on the names of its handlers. When Google changes them, the microphone stops after a second again; Windows' voice typing (Win+H) still types into the question field.
- The image search depends on the upload of Google Lens and on the number of the tab in the address of the results (`udm`). If Google changes the number, the results open on the tab "All".
- The card shows the answer as plain text: bold letters, lists and links of AI Mode come as plain lines.
- Any query button to the AI mode can choose the window or the card by its `mode` ([decision](004-one-kind-of-query.md)).
- Whether someone is signed in, the program reads from the names of Google's cookies, and its pages from `SAPISID`. If Google renames them, the program takes nobody for signed in and invites to sign in at every start and on every page.
